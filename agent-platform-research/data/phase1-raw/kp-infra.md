- Zapier and Make — workflow automation incumbents adding agents; suite-like offerings best counted at L5.
- OpenAI AgentKit, Google ADK, AWS Strands, IBM Bee — model-provider and suite agent platforms; left to the L2 and L5 sweeps to avoid double-counting.
- Modal and Baseten — general AI compute and model-serving infrastructure, not agent-specific.
- Tavus — conversational video agents; video modality adjacent, primary product is video AI.
- Salient — voice agents for auto lending; a vertical (L3) play rather than a horizontal modality platform.
- Voiceflow — conversation and agent builder spanning L2; voice is secondary.
- Fellou, Perplexity Comet, Dia — consumer agentic browsers, not enterprise platforms.
- World ID for agents and Coinbase x402 — agent identity and payment protocols tied to crypto tokens; excluded per screen.
- Smith.ai — human-plus-AI receptionist service aimed at SMBs, not an enterprise agent platform.
## Notes
- Agent security became the most crowded new L1 category: 2026 alone brought Neo's $100M launch, AIR Security's $50M, Tenet's $6M seed, Lasso's $30M and Zenity's $125M Series C, on top of Okta and Microsoft shipping agent identity products in 2025.
- The MCP tool layer consolidated: Arcade (agent authorization, $60M Series A) acquired Smithery, the leading public MCP registry, in August 2026; a micro-tier of MCP infrastructure (Glama, PulseMCP, Manufact, ToolHive) is forming around it.
- Voice is the best-funded modality: Parloa ($350M Series D at $3B), ElevenLabs ($500M ARR), LiveKit and Deepgram (both $1B+ valuations, Jan 2026), Vapi ($500M), Bland ($100M+), plus regional entrants like Ringg and Smallest.ai.
- Durable execution repositioned around agents: Temporal raised $300M at a $5B valuation (Feb 2026) while agent-native challengers (Inngest, Restate, Resonate, LittleHorse, Hatchet) remain seed-stage.
- Every global SI productized an agent platform (Accenture AI Refinery, Deloitte Zora, EY.ai Agentic Platform, KPMG Workbench, TCS and Infosys foundries); AI-native boutiques like Quantiphi and HatchWorks are far smaller but growing.
- In open source, independent frameworks (LangGraph, CrewAI, n8n at $5.2B, Dify) dominate mindshare while hyperscaler projects (AutoGen, ADK, Strands) commoditize the base layer; the seeded "Workflows AI" could not be verified as a real company.
