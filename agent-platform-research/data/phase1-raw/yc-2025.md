## Notes
- Agentic density: roughly 40% of the ~550 companies screened across W25, S25, and F25 lead with agent autonomy as the primary product; CB Insights counts 92% of F25 as AI-core, and the agentic subset grew batch over batch, with agent infrastructure (L1) expanding from ~11 companies in W25 to ~18 in F25.
- Layer mix is heavily L3: vertical work agents dominate (~147 of 216 entries), horizontal build platforms (L2) stay scarce (~10), and no L4 services firms or L5 suite products appear, as expected for YC. Healthcare, finance/accounting, GTM/sales, and legal are the most crowded verticals.
- A distinct F25 pattern is the "AI-native firm": full-stack service businesses run by software (Cranston AI and Denki in accounting/audit, Fernstone and Harper in insurance, Automax in appraisal), selling outcomes rather than seats.
- Voice agents are pervasive but mostly embedded inside vertical products (collections, hotels, pharmacies, restaurants, Medicare); only a handful sell voice as a horizontal platform (Acrely, Bolna, Leaping AI, Freya).
- Browser/computer-use agents consolidated as a modality (Browser Use, Asteroid, Magnitude, Notte, Cyberdesk, stagewise, Clicks, Dari), with open-source as the dominant go-to-market; MCP became the standard integration substrate for L1 tool-access plays (mcp-use, Metorial, Dedalus, Castari, Specific, superglue).
- Traction remains early: most entries show no visible follow-on funding or ARR; the exceptions cluster in agent infrastructure and voice (Browser Use $17M, Mastra $13M, Bolna $6.3M, Solidroad $6.5M, Leaping $4.7M, Caseflood $3.2M, Denki $4.1M, Confident AI $2.2M). Several W25/S25 companies renamed post-batch (Gulp to Osmosis, Herdora to Wafer, Dash to Slashy, Primer to Relaw, TripleZip to OpenIntake, Outrove to Sapien), so census consumers should track aliases.
