## Borderline
- Cursor (Anysphere) — AI-first IDE with agent modes; likely the largest agentic-coding business by revenue, but the product is an editor rather than an agent platform; flagged for an SWE-scope decision.
- Windsurf — AI IDE; leadership hired by Google and the remaining company acquired by Cognition (July 2025); editor-first rather than agent-first.
- GitHub Copilot, Anthropic Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Google Jules, Amazon Kiro — coding agents owned by incumbent suites and model providers; L5 territory rather than independent L3 companies.
- Kore.ai, Avaamo, Amelia, OneReach — horizontal enterprise conversational AI platforms (L2) with heavy customer service deployments.
- Bland AI, Retell AI, Vapi — horizontal voice-agent APIs; X modality infrastructure rather than vertical work agents.
- Eightfold AI, Phenom, Sense, HireVue — HR technology incumbents adding AI agent features, not agent-first products.
- BigPanda, Tines — AIOps and automation incumbents adding AI, not agent-first.
- Replit, Lovable, Bolt.new — prosumer app-builder agents, not enterprise work agents.
- NICE, Genesys, Five9, Talkdesk — CCaaS incumbents shipping agent offerings (L5); NICE also acquired Cognigy.
- Assembled, Level AI — support operations platforms adding AI features rather than agent-first products.
- Gladly, Kustomer, LivePerson — customer service suites bolting AI onto existing products.
- Ema — universal AI employees spanning departments; horizontal rather than vertical.
- Ultimate.ai — customer service agent vendor acquired by Zendesk (2024) and absorbed into Zendesk AI.
- Atlassian Rovo, ServiceNow AI Agents, Salesforce Agentforce, Microsoft Copilot Studio — L5 suite agent offerings, other sweeps.

## Notes
- Consolidation defines 2025-2026 in these verticals: ServiceNow bought Moveworks ($2.85B), NICE bought Cognigy ($955M), Zendesk bought Forethought (2026) after absorbing Ultimate (2024), Cognition absorbed Windsurf, and Salesforce took Moonhub's team as it wound down. Suites are acquiring standalone vertical agents rather than building.
- Customer service is the most mature L3 vertical: Sierra at $15B valuation, Decagon at $4.5B, Intercom Fin nearing $100M ARR, Crescendo claiming $100M+ ARR; resolution- and outcome-based pricing is becoming the norm.
- Software engineering agents show the widest dispersion: Cognition at $10.2B and CodeRabbit at $1.5B, while Sweep deprecated its product; open-source agents (OpenHands, Cline) are a genuine enterprise distribution channel.
- IT and employee-service standalones are squeezed between suites and well-funded independents: Resolve raised $125M at a $1B valuation, Traversal signed Amex, Aisera claims IDC MarketScape leadership, while Moveworks exited to ServiceNow.
- HR and recruiting is the thinnest long tail: Paradox dominates (~$116M ARR est.) while newer entrants are mostly seed-stage (Dex, Asendia, Talara, 14.ai model variants); Micro1 pivoted out to data labeling and Moonhub shut down.
- Voice is now table stakes in customer service (Parloa at $1B, PolyAI at $200M+ total, Omilia, Infinitus in healthcare); Yellow.ai is going public via a $550M SPAC explicitly to acquire the BPOs its agents replace.
- Classification judgment: Intercom Fin and Zendesk AI are layered L5 because they are the agent offerings of incumbent customer service suites, even though they were seeded as L3; TheLoops is filed under customer service (support ops) rather than IT/HR where the seed list placed it.
