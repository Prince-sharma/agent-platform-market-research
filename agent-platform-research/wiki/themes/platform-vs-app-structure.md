# Platform vs App: Where Value Pools Across the Stack (P3.7)

## Scope

Implements plan section 12 (theme P3.7): the platform-versus-app structure of the work-agent market - where value pools across the census layers (L1 runtime, L2 build platforms, L3 vertical agents, L4 services, L5 incumbent suites, X modalities), the Cloudflare edge-compute analogy as one lens among several (not a hypothesis to prove), the middle-layer question raised by the long-horizon theme (value pooling at the two ends while the middle stays thin), and what the structure implies for where a new platform entrant can capture value. Neutral structural observation throughout; no wedge recommendations (plan section 12 is explicit). Date: 2026-09-09.

## Method

- **Census:** python3 over `data/companies-with-profiles.tsv` (1,328 rows): layer counts, status, `pricing_model` and `task_horizon` cross-tabs. All counts computed, not eyeballed.
- **Wiki Facts parse:** python3 over all 766 `wiki/companies/` pages, extracting the structured `Funding:`, `Valuation:`, and `Revenue/ARR:` Facts lines. Aggregation rule: maximum figure per company per metric. Exclusions applied, matching market-sizing's Lens B rules plus two refinements: company-wide revenue removed for suites (ServiceNow's $2.96B subscription revenue, Palantir's $1B+ quarterly, Intercom's $400M total with Fin's ~$100M agent portion substituted, Forethought's cumulative-ROI figure, OpenRouter's GMV-like inference-spend run-rate, Unframe's $100M TCV); sought-not-closed valuations dropped (Harvey's $15.5B seek, Legora's $10B+ target) in favor of closed rounds ($11B Mar 2026, $5.55B Mar 2026). Funding figures exclude valuation and SPAC-price phrases found inside Funding lines (Sierra's "$10B prior round" price, Yellow.ai's ~$550M SPAC merger value).
- **Wiki reading:** full pages for Temporal, E2B, Cloudflare Agents SDK, Glean, n8n; Facts-and-decision extracts for 15 more anchors (Vercel AI SDK, OpenRouter, Exa, Braintrust, LangChain, Lovable, Zapier Agents, Palantir AIP, Sierra, Cognition, Cursor, ElevenLabs, Salesforce Agentforce, Writer, Applied Compute); the four relevant cluster pages (L1 runtime, L2 build platforms, L5 suites, X modalities).
- **Web (accessed 2026-09-09, primary first):** Cloudflare Workers and R2 pricing docs; Cloudflare Q2 2026 results press release; Vercel Active CPU pricing blog (2025-06-25); AWS Bedrock AgentCore pricing page and GA coverage (2025-07-16 preview, 2025-10-23 GA); Modal pricing page.
- **Sibling themes as quantitative base:** market-sizing.md (Lens B revenue floors, Lens C capital flows, M&A totals) and long-horizon-agents.md (the two-ends structural read this theme tests).
- **Labels:** every figure is marked reported (company/primary stated), estimated (third-party), or inferred (derived here). Private ARR is as-reported unless noted.

## Findings

### A. Where value pools: the layer-by-layer comparison

1. **Disclosed revenue concentrates at the application layer: L3 holds roughly 60% of census-visible agent revenue.** Scripted sums of maximum disclosed revenue per company, by layer:
   - L3: $2.96B across 34 disclosers - Cognition ~$900M run-rate (Sept 2026, reported); Cursor $500M+ (Sept 2026, reported); Sierra $200M (May 2026, reported); Replit $150M (Sept 2025, reported); Emergent $120M; Paradox ~$116M; then a six-company $100M cohort (Abridge, Decagon, EliseAI, Cresta, Crescendo, Harvey - mixed reported/estimated).
   - L5: $1.2B across 4 - Salesforce Agentforce $800M AI ARR (Sept 2026, reported); ServiceNow ~$200M ACV on the Pro Plus AI tier (Dec 2024, reported); Fin agent ~$100M (Aug 2026, estimated).
   - X: $543M across 5 - ElevenLabs $500M (2026, reported); Retell $40M (Nov 2025, reported).
   - L2: $450M across 2 - Aisera ~$250M (2026, estimated, since acquired); Glean $200M (Sept 2026, reported).
   - L1: $82M across 9, mostly third-party estimates - Merge ~$20M (2023, Sacra), LangSmith $16M (2025, GetLatka), LiteLLM $10M+ (2026, reported); Idler's $22.5M is flagged low-confidence on its own page.
   Cognition's ~$900M run-rate alone exceeds all disclosed L1 revenue combined by roughly 10x, and the next L3 tier is deeper than every other layer's peak.

2. **Valuations concentrate even harder at the application layer.** Peak closed valuations by layer:
   - L3: Cognition $48B (2026-09-08, reported) - the census peak.
   - X: ElevenLabs $11B (2026-02-04, reported).
   - L2: Glean $7.2B (Jun 2025, reported).
   - L1: Temporal $5B (Feb 2026, reported).
   - L5: Fin ~$3.6B agreed acquisition price (Jun 2026, reported) - the only suite "valuation" that is an agent-product mark rather than a whole-company figure (Zendesk's $10.2B is the 2022 PE take-private of the entire company).
   The L3 peak is ~10x the L1 peak and ~7x the L2 peak. Below the peaks the gradient persists: L3 holds 35 of the census's 52 known $1B+ valuations - 67% (scripted count, 2026-09-09) - against 7 in L2, 4 each in L1 and X, and 2 in L5, including Sierra $15.8B, Harvey $11B, Cursor $9.9B, Replit $9B, Clay $7B, Legora $5.55B, Abridge $5.3B, Wonderful $5B, Decagon $4.5B.

3. **Capital follows the same gradient: 74% of visible funding sits in L3.** Visible funding (max figure per company from 328 of 766 pages with a parseable Funding fact; a floor, not a total): L3 $22.3B, L1 $2.97B, X $2.49B, L2 $2.07B, L5 $250M, L4 $43M - $30.1B total. Cognition's single $2B Series E (Sept 2026) is roughly 3x Temporal's entire $650M raised and nearly equals the L2 layer's total visible funding. The gradient is not a 2026 artifact: the L1 cluster's own page records four companies at $800M+ valuation against 40+ pre-seed entrants with no visible funding - bimodal in every layer, but the L3 mode is an order of magnitude higher.

4. **The market prices the layers on different things: L3 on revenue growth, L1 on strategic position, L2 on adjacent-category heritage.** Valuation-to-ARR multiples where both are known: Harvey 110x ($11B / ~$100M estimated), Sierra 79x ($15.8B / $200M), Replit 60x, Cognition 53x, Abridge 53x, Decagon 45x - against Glean 36x (the only L2 company with both figures), ElevenLabs 22x, Cursor 20x, and Crescendo 5x (unaudited claim). L1's leaders disclose no ARR at all: Temporal ($5B), Exa ($2.2B), OpenRouter ($1.3B) carry valuations on standard-setting positions - Temporal's 1T monthly actions and 2,900+ customers, OpenRouter's ~25T tokens routed weekly - not on disclosed cash flow. LangSmith's $1.25B against a $16M GetLatka estimate (~78x) is the only L1 multiple and rests on an estimate.

5. **Acquisition premiums pay for proven work delivery, not builder tooling.** Disclosed prices (Phase 2-verified):
   - Salesforce-Fin ~$3.6B for ~$100M agent ARR (~36x, Jun 2026); ServiceNow-Moveworks $2.85B (Mar 2025); Workday-Sana $1.1B; NICE-Cognigy ~$955M; Dynatrace-Arize $915M (Aug 2026); Palo Alto-Console ~$500M; Cato-Aim Security ~$350M (Sep 2025); SoundHound-Amelia $80M - $10.4B across 8 deals, $13-18B estimated total 2024-26 (market-sizing Lens C).
   - The acquired are overwhelmingly CX and employee-support agents with outcome-based pricing (L5 cluster page); the two largest L1 exits (Arize $915M, Aim ~$350M) are observability and security capabilities absorbed into incumbent platforms - capability tuck-ins, not standalone-layer outcomes.
   - The L1 cluster's twelve-month pattern generalizes it: Aim to Cato, Pipedream to Workday, Smithery to Arcade, Arize to Dynatrace - incumbents buying observability, security, and connectivity rather than building, at prices below the L3 leaders' single rounds.

6. **Capital-efficiency proxies (inferred; the census carries no employee counts - 0 of 766 pages).** ARR per dollar of visible funding: Glean $200M / $150M = 1.3; ElevenLabs $500M / $500M = 1.0; Aisera ~$250M / $90M = 2.8 (estimated ARR, acquired); Cognition ~$900M / $3B = 0.3; Cursor $500M+ / $2.3B = 0.2; Sierra $200M / $1B = 0.2. Read with vintage caution (late mega-rounds inflate denominators), the pattern is consistent with finding 15: the efficient revenue converters either inherited an adjacent category's distribution (Glean) or own a model-like modality (ElevenLabs), while the L3 outcome leaders bought their scale with capital.

7. **The suite layer already out-earns the standalone platform layer.** Salesforce Agentforce's $800M AI ARR (Sept 2026, reported) exceeds the combined disclosed revenue of all L1 ($82M) and L2 ($450M) companies - $532M - by half again, from a standing start in late 2024. Microsoft discloses no agent split but claims 80% of the Fortune 500 run active agents (Phase 2, reported, unaudited). Suites monetize agents through installed bases and bundled control planes; no standalone builder platform has demonstrated comparable agent-specific revenue. The suite playbook is visible in their M&A direction: buying proven outcome-priced agents (Fin ~$3.6B, Moveworks $2.85B, Forethought) rather than builder tooling.

8. **The X modality layer sits economically between infrastructure and applications, closer to the model vendors.** ElevenLabs pairs an $11B valuation with $500M ARR (22x) on subscription-plus-usage voice - model-like economics with a platform wrapped around it; Parloa ($3B, Jan 2026) sells enterprise voice CX into Fortune 200 contact centers; Deepgram ($1.3B) and LiveKit ($1B) are the infrastructure pair (speech models and WebRTC transport). Pricing is 100% usage-based across all 15 known X models (scripted cross-tab) - the layer behaves like L1 in pricing and like L3 in revenue concentration, because owning the modality model (not the runtime) is what captures the margin.

### B. The Cloudflare analogy, as one lens among several

9. **What Cloudflare productized, precisely: stateful compute as a metered utility at the edge.** Workers at $0.30 per additional million requests and $0.02 per million CPU-milliseconds with 10M requests included (developers.cloudflare.com, accessed 2026-09-09); R2 object storage at $0.015/GB-month with zero egress fees; Durable Objects for stateful coordination. The commercial yield of productizing that layer: Cloudflare's Q2 2026 revenue of $696.1M, +36% YoY (press release, 2026-08-06) - roughly $2.8B annualized, after a decade-plus of building.

10. **The agent-runtime equivalents now exist and price identically - the analogy's mechanical half is real.** E2B: per-second sandboxes, CPU $0.000014-0.000112/vCPU-s (e2b.dev/pricing, accessed 2026-09-09). AWS Bedrock AgentCore: per-second CPU and memory consumption billing, memory $0.00945/GiB-hour (preview 2025-07-16, GA 2025-10-23). Vercel Fluid Compute: Active CPU from $0.128/vCPU-hour (blog, 2025-06-25). Modal: $0.0000131/core-second CPU, GPUs $0.000583-0.001972/second by card (modal.com/pricing, accessed 2026-09-09). Cloudflare's own Agents SDK ships durable execution, scheduling, and built-in RAG on the Workers runtime - 500k weekly downloads as of April 2026 (wiki, reported). Temporal ($5B) sells platform fee plus metered actions. The metered-utility playbook has been adopted wholesale.

11. **Where the analogy breaks - on the plan's two stated breaks, both visible in the census data.**
    - **Agents bind to data, workflows, and integrations, not just compute.** The L1 cluster's own patterns make integration the moat (MCP as standard substrate; proprietary connector libraries where normalization matters), and the deep verticals integrate precisely because no platform provides the bindings: Basis built its own ledger/ERP connectors, Harvey built Vault, Sierra built its own memory layer, Trunk Tools built Cortex (long-horizon theme, Q3). Cloudflare never had to solve "your compute must read the customer's ERP"; agent runtimes do.
    - **Enterprises buy outcomes, not runtimes.** L1 has zero per-outcome pricing - the cluster page states it does not exist there; everything is metered on inputs (tokens, calls, hours). The outcome-priced businesses (Sierra per-resolution, Fin $0.99, Crescendo $1.25, Agentforce $0.10/action) are applications and suites. The buyer's unit of value is the resolved ticket or merged PR, not the sandbox-second.
    - A third break the data adds: **the compute-shaped slice is commoditizing under the hyperscalers.** AgentCore is priced like any other AWS compute; Cloudflare and Vercel effectively give the agent runtime away to sell compute. The independent runtime companies survive on OSS distribution and standard-setting (E2B's 88%-of-Fortune-100 claim and Apache-2.0 core; Temporal's MIT core), which converts to thin visible revenue (finding 1).

12. **Honest assessment: the agent-runtime layer is productizing like edge compute did - metered, per-second, utility-priced - but the productized slice is the compute-shaped subset of agents, and that subset is a small revenue pool.** L1's census-visible revenue is $60-80M (market-sizing Lens B) against $2.97B of visible funding; the whole platform-plus-infrastructure SAM is $2-6B in 2026 (market-sizing, estimated). Edge compute's addressable base was all software compute; the standalone agent-runtime base is the metered-execution slice, while the binding layer (data, integrations, governance) and the outcome layer hold the value the Cloudflare analogy implies. The analogy is a correct description of the pricing architecture and an unreliable guide to where the money pools.

13. **Two structural differences the pricing data makes concrete.**
    - **The runtime is free where it sells someone else's compute.** Cloudflare's Agents SDK and Vercel's AI SDK cost nothing - the runtime is a demand generator for Workers and Fluid compute, exactly as R2's zero egress sells storage. AWS prices AgentCore like any other EC2-adjacent service. An independent runtime vendor therefore competes with free offerings from every hyperscaler plus the model vendors' free SDKs (OpenAI AgentKit, Anthropic Agent SDK, AWS Strands) - the OSS-core strategy (E2B Apache-2.0 at 13.6k stars, Temporal MIT, roughly 40% of L1 shipping an OSS core) is the counter-move, and Phase 2's finding holds: OSS converts to infrastructure businesses, not vertical ones.
    - **The runtime's customer is the builder, not the enterprise.** E2B is embedded in Manus, Perplexity, Groq, Lindy, Gumloop, Hugging Face (wiki); Applied Compute serves Cognition, Harvey, DoorDash; Arcade acquired Smithery to own the agent tool supply chain. Cloudflare's edge customers were the developers shipping their own software; the agent runtime sells to application companies who capture the outcome revenue - so runtime revenue is a small percentage of the outcome revenue it enables, by construction.

### C. The middle-layer question

14. **The two-ends finding holds, and the middle is thinner than its headline valuations.** The long-horizon theme's read - value pooling at the durable substrate (Temporal $5B) and the outcome-priced verticals (Sierra $15.8B, Cognition $48B) with the middle thin - survives the layer data: L2's peak valuation (Glean $7.2B) sits between the ends, and L2's only current, independent, reported agent-adjacent ARR is Glean's $200M. But the boundary needs precision: the middle is not empty, it is occupied by adjacent categories wearing agent clothing.

15. **L2's headline valuations are carried by adjacent-category heritage, not agent-platform revenue.** Glean is an enterprise knowledge platform - search, assistants, then agents - grounded in 250+ proprietary connectors (wiki page; its $200M ARR is knowledge-plus-agents). n8n is workflow automation (founded pre-agent, fair-code license, 500+ integrations) whose $5.2B (May 2026) follows a strategic SAP investment. Lovable ($2.8B) is a natural-language app builder. Palantir AIP's $1B+ quarterly revenue (2026, reported) is company-wide across ontology software and government contracts, not agent-platform revenue. The model-vendor SDKs (OpenAI AgentKit, Anthropic Agent SDK, Vercel AI SDK, AWS Strands) are free - they monetize model consumption, not platform use. The agent-native L2 stratum (LangChain ~$1.3B, Applied Compute $1.3B, Ema, Gumloop, Dify, Mastra) tops out below $1.5B.

16. **The exit wave confirms the middle's instability rather than its strength.** In roughly twelve months: Aisera into Automation Anywhere, Sana ($1.1B) and Flowise into Workday, Langflow into IBM via DataStax, Yellow.ai SPAC at ~$550M, Adept's team absorbed by Amazon (L2 cluster page; Phase 2). Middle-layer companies are exiting into suites and RPA incumbents at single-digit billions or less, while the application leaders (Cognition, Sierra, Cursor) raise at $10-48B. The market's verdict on standalone middle-layer independence is visible in the price gap.

17. **What the middle layer's own assessment says it lacks: governance as infrastructure and outcome-native economics.** The L2 cluster page names two open grounds: governance as a primary infrastructure layer rather than a feature (the suites' second-wave control planes - Agent 365, AI Control Tower, Digital Wallet - are ahead of the startups on exactly this), and platforms optimized for the AI-native firm that sells outcomes rather than seats (the newest census pattern, Phase 2 section 3.11). Both gaps point the same direction: the middle's unfinished product is the governed binding between enterprise data and agent work, priced in outcome units - which is also what the pricing theme identifies as the unclaimed surface and the long-horizon theme as the missing platform.

### D. What the structure implies

18. **Neutral structural read for where a new platform entrant can capture value.** Four observations, no recommendation:
    - The **metered-runtime slice is commoditizing**: per-second pricing from four hyperscaler-adjacent providers plus OSS cores means execution alone is not a value pool; it is a feature of someone else's cloud.
    - The **binding layer - data, integrations, governance, memory - is where L2's durable value actually sits** (Glean's connectors, n8n's integration ecosystem), but it is contested from above by the suites' second-wave control planes (Agent 365, AI Control Tower, Digital Wallet) and from below by MCP standardizing tool access.
    - The **outcome layer is where revenue, valuation, and M&A premiums concentrate**, but it requires vertical depth (connectors, workflow knowledge, liability posture) that horizontal platforms have not provided - the repeated vertical integration by Basis, Harvey, Sierra, Trunk Tools is the census's strongest signal of an unmet platform demand (long-horizon theme, Q3).
    - **The suites' bundling is the structural pincer**: Agentforce's $800M from a standing start shows installed-base distribution converting to agent revenue faster than any standalone platform's, while Copilot Studio and Agent 365 own the governance surface. What would change this read: the plan's own listed triggers - suites pricing builders near zero, MCP commoditizing the build layer, or per-outcome pricing generalizing beyond CX.

## Implications

- **For the central question - what an agent-building platform must provide:** the value map says the platform layer that enterprises will pay for is not the runtime (commoditizing, per-second, often free from hyperscalers) but the binding and governance layer - data, integrations, memory, approval gates, spend control - assembled with the outcome economics buyers actually purchase. The census's vertical leaders keep rebuilding exactly those six primitives (long-horizon theme), which is the demand signal for a complete platform; the census's platform layer keeps not owning them, which is why its revenue is thin.
- **The Cloudflare analogy is half right and the wrong half is instructive.** The metered-utility pricing architecture has been adopted everywhere (E2B, AgentCore, Fluid, Modal, Temporal) - but Cloudflare productized a layer whose consumers were developers shipping their own software; agent platforms serve buyers who purchase finished work. The runtime productizes; the revenue pools elsewhere. A Cloudflare-scale outcome for the agent layer would require the binding layer to become the metered substrate - priced per outcome or per governed workflow, not per sandbox-second - which no one currently does (per-outcome pricing is absent from all of L1).
- **The middle layer's strategic position is real but its economics are inherited.** Glean and n8n are the proof that the middle monetizes when it brings an adjacent category's distribution (enterprise search, workflow automation) - and the exit wave (Sana, Flowise, Langflow, Aisera, Yellow.ai) is the proof that agent-native middles without that heritage get absorbed. The suites' control planes are the middle's most direct competitor, and they are ahead on governance (Phase 2 section 3.8).
- **The modality layer is the quiet exception.** ElevenLabs ($11B / $500M, 22x) shows that owning a frontier modality model with a platform wrapped around it captures infrastructure-grade revenue at application-grade multiples - the one place outside L3 where a standalone company pairs a $10B+ valuation with nine-figure ARR.
- **Where a new entrant can structurally capture value (observation, not recommendation):** not in metered execution; plausibly in the binding/governance layer if it can be made the purchasing unit (the "agent dollar" fragmentation the pricing theme identifies is the open surface); and in the outcome layer only with vertical depth. The single biggest uncertainty is whether the platform layer's share of enterprise AI spend stays ~2% (Menlo 2025) or climbs toward Gartner's ~15% app-dev share - an 8x spread that dominates every projection (market-sizing open questions).
- **What would falsify this read:** suites pricing credible building at near-zero marginal cost; MCP plus open frameworks commoditizing the build layer faster than platforms can add binding value; or a standalone agent-runtime company converting standard-setting into Cloudflare-scale revenue - Temporal's revenue trajectory (reported roughly doubled in 2026, The New Stack, Aug 2026, medium confidence) is the leading indicator to watch.

## Open questions

- **L1 revenue is nearly invisible by construction.** 9 of 135 L1 pages disclose any ARR, all small and mostly estimated. Whether Temporal, Exa, and OpenRouter are pre-revenue-scale or simply private means the substrate's true yield is unmeasurable from public data.
- **The middle layer's revenue is structurally underdisclosed.** Only 2 of 74 L2 pages disclose any ARR (Glean, Aisera) versus 34 of 467 in L3 - the layer claiming to be where platforms get built publishes almost no platform revenue, so its economics are inferred from valuations and exits rather than observed.
- **The valuation/ARR multiples mix vintages.** Harvey's 110x pairs a Mar 2026 valuation with a 2025-26 estimated ARR; Cursor's 20x pairs Sept 2026 figures. Directionally robust, individually noisy.
- **Suite agent revenue is self-defined.** "AI ARR" (Salesforce), "ACV on Pro Plus" (ServiceNow), and Microsoft's non-disclosure are not comparable units; the $1.2B L5 floor could be materially higher or lower in like-for-like terms.
- **Employee counts are absent from all 766 pages**, so revenue-per-employee could only be proxied by ARR-per-funding-dollar (finding 6); a headcount sweep would sharpen the capital-efficiency read.
- **The binding layer's independence is unresolved**: whether MCP plus suite control planes absorb it, or whether a standalone governance/binding platform emerges, is the open question the whole platform-vs-app structure turns on.
- **Applied Compute ($1.3B, Sep 2026) straddles the taxonomy** - eval harnesses serving Cognition, Harvey, and DoorDash are priced like infrastructure but valued like an outcome business; the L1/L2 boundary may be drawn in the wrong place for evals.
