## The eleven build decisions

1. **Build interface:** No-code/low-code framework for rapid prototyping and customization of agent networks.
2. **Runtime and tenancy:** Flexible deployment across cloud, data center, or edge (via NVIDIA AI Enterprise infrastructure).
3. **Tool and integration strategy:** Integration with third-party agent networks, NVIDIA NIM microservices, and existing enterprise systems via APIs.
4. **Knowledge and data strategy:** Industry-specific LLMs fine-tuned via NVIDIA NeMo; integration of IT-OT data and datasets for observability.
5. **Autonomy and human-in-the-loop:** Adaptive, real-time systems featuring security guardrails and human oversight.
6. **Governance and enterprise controls:** Enterprise-grade governance including security guardrails and human-in-the-loop oversight.
7. **Pricing model:** unknown
8. **GTM motion:** Services-led; blending platform accelerators (Neuro AI) with professional services for redesign and deployment.
9. **Moat thesis:** Distribution via global SI footprint and deep NVIDIA technical integration (NIM/Omniverse).
10. **Open-source posture:** Neuro AI Multi-Agent Accelerator is open-sourced under a research license.
11. **Task horizon:** Multi-step workflows and persistent agent networks for functions like supply chain and claims processing.

## Analysis

- **Agent capability depth:** Orchestrates multi-agent networks for end-to-end business processes (sales, marketing, supply chain) and industrial digital twins for manufacturing.
- **Weaknesses:** Heavy reliance on NVIDIA's underlying infrastructure (NIM/NeMo) for core platform capabilities.
- **Trajectory:** Moving from PoCs to large-scale "agent factory" deployments and industry-specific foundational models.
- **M&A:** none visible
