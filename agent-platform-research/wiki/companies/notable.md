# Notable

**Thesis (one line):** AI agents automating administrative healthcare workflows across patient access, revenue cycle management, and care operations to reduce staff burden.

## The eleven build decisions

1. **Build interface:** low-code design tool (Flow Builder) and in-platform assistant (Flow AI)
2. **Runtime and tenancy:** vendor multi-tenant SaaS
3. **Tool and integration strategy:** proprietary connector library (Epic, Oracle, Athena, HL7 MDM/ORU/ORM, SFTP, SIP)
4. **Knowledge and data strategy:** live sync with systems of record; OCR for fax/PDF transcription
5. **Autonomy and human-in-the-loop:** approval gates (warm-transfers for complex issues); guardrails (95-98% accuracy rates cited)
6. **Governance and enterprise controls:** unknown
7. **Pricing model:** unknown
8. **GTM motion:** sales-led
9. **Moat thesis:** integrations (deep EHR connectivity), workflow lock-in, and healthcare-specific data gravity
10. **Open-source posture:** proprietary
11. **Task horizon:** multi-step workflow (minutes to days, e.g., referral to booking)

## Analysis

- **Agent capability depth:** End-to-end automation of patient access (fax transcription, registration, scheduling), RCM (copay estimation, prior auth, appeal letters), and care ops (chart scrubbing, care gap closure).
- **Weaknesses:** High dependency on legacy EHR integration stability; reliance on OCR accuracy for unstructured fax data.
- **Trajectory:** Expanding from simple RPA-based administrative tasks to complex, multi-modal AI agents (Voice/SMS) that handle patient-facing interactions.
- **M&A:** none visible
