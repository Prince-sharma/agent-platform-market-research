**Thesis (one line):** AI agents automating the end-to-end pharma market access pipeline from clinical prescription to patient treatment fulfillment.

## The eleven build decisions

1. **Build interface:** unknown
2. **Runtime and tenancy:** vendor multi-tenant SaaS
3. **Tool and integration strategy:** proprietary; interacts with portals, documents, phone calls, and faxes
4. **Knowledge and data strategy:** built-in RAG; utilizes clinical models and historical case data
5. **Autonomy and human-in-the-loop:** fully autonomous agents acting as case managers; high accuracy bar for clinical safety
6. **Governance and enterprise controls:** unknown
7. **Pricing model:** platform fee/partnership (biopharma)
8. **GTM motion:** sales-led (biopharma) and organic adoption (providers)
9. **Moat thesis:** network effects (provider/payer/pharmacy connections) and data gravity from millions of cases
10. **Open-source posture:** proprietary
11. **Task horizon:** long-horizon (days to weeks)

## Analysis

- **Agent capability depth:** Agents act as autonomous case managers for each prescription. They perform benefits verification, complete payer-specific prior authorization (PA) forms, draft clinically grounded appeal letters, route prescriptions to pharmacies, and manage patient enrollment in financial assistance programs.
- **Weaknesses:** High dependency on the stability of legacy healthcare infrastructure (faxes/portals) which are outside their control.
- **Trajectory:** Expanding from a tool for providers into a comprehensive "AI network for medicine" that provides biopharma companies with real-time market access insights to accelerate drug launches.
- **M&A:** none visible
